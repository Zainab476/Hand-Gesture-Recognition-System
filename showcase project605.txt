1. The "Elevator Pitch" (Start with this)
"I built a real-time hand gesture recognition system that allows users to control a computer or robotic interface using just their hands and a standard webcam. It uses computer vision to track hand landmarks and a deep neural network to classify 8 different gestures in real-time, running entirely on consumer hardware."
2. The Project Idea (The "Why")
The Problem: Traditional human-computer interaction relies on physical devices like keyboards and mice, which aren't always practical (e.g., in robotics, sterile environments, or for users with physical disabilities).
The Solution: I wanted to create a contactless, hands-free interface. The goal was to build a system that is intuitive and runs on a standard laptop webcam without needing expensive depth sensors or wearable gloves.
3. Type of Project
This is an End-to-End Computer Vision and Deep Learning Project.
It is not just a Jupyter Notebook with a trained model. It is a full software application. You handled the entire lifecycle:
Data generation & preprocessing.
Feature engineering (normalization).
Neural network architecture & training.
Automated testing (PyTest).
Real-time deployment and UI rendering.
4. How It Works (The Pipeline)
When they ask, "How does your system actually work?", explain it in these 5 steps:
Video Capture: I used OpenCV to capture frames from the webcam.
Landmark Detection: I passed the frames to MediaPipe, which detects the hand and extracts 21 3D coordinates (x, y, z) for the finger joints and wrist.
Feature Normalization (Crucial Step!): To make sure the model works whether the user's hand is close to or far from the camera, I translated the wrist coordinate to the origin (0,0,0) and scaled all other points based on the maximum distance between joints. This gave me a 63-element feature vector (21 points × 3 dimensions) that is scale-invariant.
Classification: I fed this normalized vector into a Deep Neural Network (TensorFlow/Keras) to predict one of the 8 gestures.
Real-Time Rendering: Finally, OpenCV draws the hand skeleton on the screen and overlays the predicted gesture, confidence score, and FPS.
5. Tools & Tech Stack (And why you used them)
Python: The core language.
MediaPipe: For ultra-fast, accurate hand tracking. (Interviewer tip: Mention you chose MediaPipe because it's optimized for real-time performance on CPUs, unlike heavier models).
TensorFlow / Keras: For building and training the Deep Neural Network.
OpenCV: For webcam access and drawing the UI overlays.
Scikit-learn & NumPy: For data preprocessing (StandardScaler) and calculating evaluation metrics.
PyTest: (Highlight this!) "I wrote automated unit and integration tests to ensure every stage of my pipeline—like data loading and normalization—worked correctly before going live."

To truly convince them you built this, you need to talk about the results, the challenges, and the future improvements. Here is your cheat sheet:
🏆 Talk about your Wins (Metrics)
"The model achieved 86.87% overall accuracy. Out of the 8 gestures, 6 of them (like Fist, OK Sign, and Peace Sign) achieved a perfect 100% F1-score. The system ran at a stable 5-6 FPS in real-time, which is smooth enough for interactive control."
🚧 Talk about the Challenge (They WILL ask this)
Interviewer: "What was the hardest part of this project?"
You: "The biggest challenge was that the model frequently confused the 'Open Hand' and 'Stop Palm' gestures. Because both gestures involve all five fingers being extended, their 2D/3D landmark structures look almost identical to the model. Since I used a synthetic dataset, it lacked the subtle depth and wrist-rotation nuances of real human hands."
🚀 Talk about Future Improvements (Shows a Growth Mindset)
Interviewer: "How would you fix that issue?"
You: "If I were to take this to production, I would do three things:
Collect a real-user dataset with varied lighting and backgrounds.
*Add temporal modeling (like an LSTM or Transformer). Instead of looking at a single static frame, the model would look at the sequence of frames to understand the movement and depth of the wrist.*
Apply model quantization so it could be deployed on edge devices like a Raspberry Pi or a microcontroller."
